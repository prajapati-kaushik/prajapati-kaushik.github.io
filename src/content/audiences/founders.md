## Why this might be you

You already have a product that customers pay for. It runs on PHP, or on an API-first stack where PHP is one of the pieces. The codebase is older than you would like, the person who wrote it has moved on, and every new feature feels riskier than the last. You need someone who can take the backend off your plate without stopping the business.

## What I have done that looks like your problem

- Kept a Dutch parent portal used by millions of new parents available while its backend was replaced. Purchases still turned into access, intake and pregnancy-week email still arrived, and the API stayed compatible for the apps already using it.
- Kept payments and invoices consistent while a parent buys something — the paid record is never silently rewritten, and the activation step knows what was bought.
- Moved older PHP products — mail, alerting, internal tools — onto Symfony without a shutdown. A parenting product's email kept sending after its old provider was replaced.
- Added small integrations the business needed quickly: an invoice-generation endpoint for the finance workflow, a webhook flow for account changes, with the request and the documentation in the same week.

## What you get in the first month

1. A written map of the system as it is: what calls what, where money and email move, what is fragile.
2. The first slice shipped behind a compatible interface, so you can see the method before you commit to the programme.
3. Root-cause write-ups for whatever is currently hurting, with the fix and the query attached.
4. A plan in plain language that you, not only another engineer, can read and challenge.

## How I work with founders

- I ask what the business needs before I ask what the code needs.
- I ship in small, tested increments. You see progress weekly, not at the end of a quarter.
- I write things down. Decisions, runbooks, and handovers live in the repo, not in my head.
- I use AI tooling where it speeds up analysis and migration drafts, and I review everything it produces.

## Fit

**Good fit:** seed-to-growth product companies on PHP/Symfony or API-first B2B, healthtech or tools especially, that will let one engineer own a domain from API to data to deployment. Contract, B2B, or a long-term role.

**Not a fit:** "move fast and skip the tests", meeting-heavy founder-led standups, US Pacific-only overlap, or a hire whose job is to abandon PHP in week one.
